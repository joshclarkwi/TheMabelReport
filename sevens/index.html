      Appendix

      The Rules

      1. Two to eight players. The deck is dealt out as evenly as it goes, so with an awkward number some hands carry one extra card. The deal rotates, so that falls on somebody new each time.
      2. Whoever holds the seven of spades opens with it.
      3. Spades set the pace. A spade extends the spade row by one rank either way — down toward the ace, up toward the king.
      4. A card of any other suit may only be laid once the spade of the same rank is already down. The eight of hearts waits on the eight of spades; the four of clubs waits on the four of spades. It must still follow its own row, one rank at a time from its seven.
      5. You must play if you can.
      6. If you have no legal card, you must ask the player who last laid a card for one. They choose which card to hand over, and it is seen only by the two of you. You take it into your hand and forfeit your turn, playing again when your turn next comes round.
      7. First to empty their hand wins the deal.

      Spades are the engine of the whole game — nothing else moves until they do. Hold one back and you throttle the entire table, your own hand along with it.

      Choosing what to give is its own small cruelty. Hand over the card you are least likely to be rid of and you are usually handing them the same problem.

      Play goes round the table one seat at a time and nobody ever lays two cards in a row. But a forfeited turn puts no card on the table, so when several players in a row have nothing to play it can look as though your turn never came. The strip along the top shows whose turn it is; the running note above your hand records what has just happened, including every turn of your own.

      Computer players are marked ◇. The host may seat up to seven of them, and may start the game whenever they please without waiting for a full table. Should a player's telephone go quiet mid-game, the house plays their hand until they return.
